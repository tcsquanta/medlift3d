\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Lung nodules are followed up by measuring how fast they grow, but computed tomography (CT), the standard tool, gives the patient a radiation dose and a cost at every follow-up, while low-dose digital chest tomosynthesis (DTS) cannot resolve depth because its \ang{30} sweep leaves a ``missing wedge'' of unmeasured information. MedLift-3D is a click-to-3D system that turns one click on a DTS slice into a 3D nodule measurement with a calibrated uncertainty. We first show from projection moments that every DTS view measures a nodule's mass exactly, while its depth extent, which volume needs, is about 15 times worse conditioned; mass and mass doubling time are therefore the measurements DTS can support. The pipeline simulates low-dose 15-view DTS for 583 LIDC-IDRI patients (1,144 radiologist-annotated nodules), reconstructs a \SI{64}{mm} prism through the chest around the click, fills the missing wedge with a 17.4-million-parameter conditional 3D diffusion model kept consistent with the X-rays, represents the nodule with 3D Gaussians that give its mass in closed form, and wraps every number in conformal prediction intervals and calibrated size-category probabilities. A learned 3D detector suggests where to click, and a seen/unseen (null-space) analysis explains which part of each measurement comes from the X-rays and which from the learned prior. On held-out patients the diffusion prior raised nodule-region PSNR from \SI{11.88}{dB} to \SI{15.23}{dB} without overfitting (held-out to training loss ratio 0.97), and the detector reached a CPM of 0.298 against 0.017 for a training-free baseline. \fillme{one sentence with the H1 result (mass vs volume error) and one with the coverage of the 90\% intervals}. The full study ran on one rented GPU in 14 hours for about US\$16, and an interactive demo runs on a free Kaggle notebook.

\vspace{1em}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} digital tomosynthesis, lung nodule volumetry, diffusion models, 3D Gaussians, conformal prediction, explainable AI
