\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Lung nodules are followed up by measuring how fast they grow, but computed tomography (CT), the standard tool, gives the patient a radiation dose and a cost at every follow-up, while low-dose digital chest tomosynthesis (DTS) cannot resolve depth because its \ang{30} sweep leaves a ``missing wedge'' of unmeasured information. MedLift-3D is a click-to-3D system that turns one click on a DTS slice into a 3D nodule measurement with a calibrated uncertainty. We first show from projection moments that every DTS view measures a nodule's mass exactly, while its depth extent, which volume needs, is about 15 times worse conditioned; mass and mass doubling time are therefore the measurements DTS can support. The pipeline simulates low-dose 15-view DTS for 583 LIDC-IDRI patients (1,144 radiologist-annotated nodules), reconstructs a \SI{64}{mm} prism through the chest around the click, fills the missing wedge with a 17.4-million-parameter conditional 3D diffusion model kept consistent with the X-rays, represents the nodule with 3D Gaussians that give its mass in closed form, and wraps every number in conformal prediction intervals and calibrated size-category probabilities. A learned 3D detector suggests where to click, and a seen/unseen (null-space) analysis explains which part of each measurement comes from the X-rays and which from the learned prior. The system components worked as specified: the diffusion prior raised nodule-region PSNR on held-out data from \SI{11.88}{dB} to \SI{15.23}{dB}, the detector reached a CPM of 0.298 against 0.017 for a training-free baseline, conformal intervals reached 95\% coverage on 74 test nodules, and calibrated category probabilities lowered the Brier score for a volume of at least \SI{100}{mm^3} from 0.54 to 0.17. The null-space analysis confirmed that the X-rays measure almost all of a nodule's mass (0.3\% unseen) and that the prior's unseen content correlates with the truth (skill 0.22). The measurements, however, did not reach the goal: mass was not more accurate than volume, the full method's volume error (MdAPE 94.6\%) was not lower than physics-only reconstruction (87.7--91.2\%), and the valid intervals were too wide to be useful. We trace these failures to a prior that shrinks faint nodules, to measuring mass in image space rather than from the projections, and to failed measurements inside the calibration set, and show that better image quality did not imply better measurement. An interactive demo runs on a free Kaggle notebook.

\vspace{1em}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} digital tomosynthesis, lung nodule volumetry, diffusion models, 3D Gaussians, conformal prediction, explainable AI
